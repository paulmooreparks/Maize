# Maize v2 Phase 2 drafting ledger

This document records every design call the Phase 2 drafting fleet made that the
ratified decision record and the drafted backbone did not fix. It exists for the
Phase 3 review lanes: each entry is a decision an agent made, accepted by the
orchestrator, and each is fair game for adversarial review. The decision record at
maize2-decisions.md remains the sole ratified authority; nothing here amends it.

The backbone's own twenty-three calls are recorded on the campaign card and
restated in the chapters themselves. The entries below are the chapter agents'.

## execution-model, register-model

- Program counter wraparound: advancing past the highest address wraps modulo 2^64 and yields address zero rather than raising a distinct fault; accessibility of the resulting address is deferred to the memory-model chapter. Neither the record nor the backbone fixed this.
- Fault-versus-interrupt priority at an instruction boundary: a fault or trap raised by an instruction is delivered before any interrupt pending at that boundary, and the interrupt stays pending for the next deliverable boundary. Which of several simultaneously pending interrupts wins is left explicitly to the trap-model chapter.
- Block-memory instructions are interruptible at a byte boundary, not only at their own instruction boundaries, so a long copy cannot delay an interrupt for an unbounded time. The inventory fixed the restart state but not the interrupt granularity.
- wait_for_interrupt race semantics: executing it with an interrupt already pending and deliverable completes immediately and the interrupt is taken at the following boundary, so no interrupt can be lost by racing to sleep.
- Three named execution states (running, waiting, halted) as the vocabulary for machine status, with the unhandled-trap outcome explicitly delegated to the trap-model chapter rather than made a fourth state.
- Reset state and process start are deliberately NOT defined in the execution-model chapter; the chapter states in one sentence that initial CSR state, the entry address, and the boot-information block belong to the privileged-architecture and memory-model chapters. v1 put reset in its execution-model chapter, so this is a scope change that another chapter must pick up.
- The phrase 'the address of the following instruction' is given one spec-wide definition (first byte plus encoded length, computed before execution) covering pc_add, call, branch and jump displacement bases, and breakpoint.
- Determinism is bounded by exactly two named non-pure categories (device interactions through ports and interrupts, and block_copy's implementation-chosen transfer order), with an explicit statement that no third category exists.
- Register-model naming: only the three architecturally justified aliases (zero, ra, sp) are defined here; the remaining D4 ABI alias spellings (argument and temporary register names) are deliberately left to the calling-convention chapter rather than invented, since the decision record names none of them.
- Positional-name bit ranges are tabulated explicitly with index 0 at the least significant end, justified by little-endianness and agreement with store byte order. The encoding chapter fixed the form-field indices but not the bit ranges.
- A dotted name over r0 obeys the zero register: extracting any field of r0 yields zero and inserting into any field of r0 is discarded, because the hardwiring applies before the positional machinery.
- r30 has no architecture-guaranteed reset value, stated explicitly as part of the stack-pointer-by-convention-only rule.
- Trap entry is stated not to touch r31, as a corollary of the four-word frame saving no general register.

## instruction-reference-integer

- Entry granularity: one entry per canonical mnemonic, with its width-modified (.h) and immediate forms listed as additional Syntax lines rather than as separate entries. The inventory and the opcode map keep them separate; collapsing them here avoids restating identical rules 46 times. Stated explicitly in the chapter's 'Reading an entry' section.
- Divide-error subcode naming: the inventory names only the 'quotient-overflow' subcode. I named the zero-divisor case the 'divide-by-zero' subcode so both arms of the trap have a pinnable name. The trap-model chapter must adopt these two subcode names or correct mine.
- remainder_signed of the most negative value by -1 writes zero and raises no trap. The inventory's shared rule says only that 'the signed DIVISION of the most negative value by -1' raises quotient-overflow; the remainder is exactly zero and is representable, so trapping it would be gratuitous. This diverges from x86 idiv and matches RISC-V. Called out again in the conformance notes.
- A trapping instruction writes nothing: destination registers hold their prior values, which follows from the trap model's restartability contract but was not stated for the ALU family. Stated once in the chapter preamble and repeated on each divide Traps line.
- The decode-stage illegal-operand trap (nonzero form field in a plain slot) applies to every instruction in the chapter and is stated once in the preamble; per-entry Traps lines therefore cover execution-stage traps only, which is what lets most of them read 'None'.
- Immediate handling in .h forms: the 32-bit literal IS the half-word operand directly, with no extension applied, since literal and operation are the same width. The inventory says ALU immediates are 'sign-extended', which is vacuous at .h width; I made the non-extension explicit.
- Shift-count literal signedness: the 8-bit shift-count immediate is masked before use, so its signedness never arises and the spec does not assign one.
- pc_add does not translate, validate, or access the address it computes, and wraps modulo 2^64; a bad address faults at the later use, not here. Also fixed that pc_add #0 yields the pc_add's own address plus 6, since the instruction is six bytes.
- multiply has no signed/unsigned pair because the low half of a product is signedness-independent; multiply_high_signed and multiply_high_unsigned have no .h forms because the full product of two half-words fits in a word. Both stated as rationale in the entries.
- negate of the most negative value returns that value and raises no trap (falls out of the wrap rule, but the corner deserved a sentence).
- Compares have no .h forms, and a SIGNED comparison of .h results requires sign-extending the operands first (extract.sh or bitfield_extract_signed) because .h arithmetic zero-extends. Named as a compiler obligation, not a machine behavior. This is the practical edge of D5's zero-extension ruling and no drafted document had stated it.
- and/or/xor have no .h forms, because a bitwise operation on the low half is the word operation with a masked literal. Consistent with the opcode map, which assigns none, but the reason was unstated.
- Carry-out canonical form: the carry-out written to rc is 0 or 1 with bits 63 through 1 cleared, so a carry register is canonical after the first limb. The inventory said only that bit 0 is READ; the write side needed pinning.
- subtract_borrow's borrow-out is defined as 1 exactly when rs2 plus the incoming borrow exceeds rs1 as an unsigned computation (the x86/ARM borrow polarity, not the inverted-carry convention).
- add_carry / subtract_borrow have no .h forms, because a multi-precision chain is built from full-width limbs.
- Seven conformance-note bullets added at the chapter foot, each phrased as a property a binary can execute and check.

## overview, terminology

- Chapter list in the reader's map: neither the decision record nor the backbone fixes the specification's table of contents, so I named fifteen chapter subjects plus appendices (terminology, register model, instruction encoding, memory model, execution model, instruction inventory, floating point, trap model, privileged architecture, system interface, device surface, extensions, conformance, cost model, appendices). The names 'system-interface chapter' (syscall boundary plus calling convention, covering D13 and D4 together) and 'device-surface chapter' are mine; if the fleet split the ABI out as its own chapter or titled these differently, the map needs a one-line reconciliation in the later link pass.
- Overview declares itself framing rather than normative and says the later chapter governs on any apparent disagreement, mirroring v1's chapter 1 stance. No decision fixed whether the overview is normative.
- Terminology fixes bit numbering as 0 at the least significant bit, with inclusive ranges written 'bits 15 through 0'. The encoding chapter uses that convention already but does not state it.
- Terminology fixes negative-literal spelling as the minus sign AFTER the base marker (#-1, $-20). D11 mandates base markers but does not place the sign; the inventory's 'compare_lt_unsigned r4 $-1 r5' example is consistent with this call.
- Terminology fixes two lexical points the record leaves open: a semicolon begins a line comment, and a label is an identifier followed by a colon at the start of a line. Both follow v1 mazm.
- Terminology fixes the four normative terms 'traps', 'reserved', 'defined', and 'privileged', and states the conformance-testability test as the criterion for what may appear in a normative chapter.
- Overview's worked example (sum_words) is my own; it uses only forms present in the inventory (move register form, branch_eq/branch_ne, bare-form load, ALU immediate second-source forms, return) and returns in r2 per D4's argument-and-result register map.
- Terminology states that the disassembler emits the calling convention's register names, extending D2's 'disassembler emits the ABI names' into the spec's own prose without using the term ABI in that sentence.
- Overview states that instruction length runs one to ten bytes in the base and never exceeds sixteen anywhere; both numbers come from the encoding chapter, but the summary framing is mine.

## memory-model

- Boot-information block signature: the eight ASCII bytes M A I Z E B I B at offset $00, chosen as a byte sequence rather than an integer magic so no reader reasons about the byte order of the magic itself.
- Boot-information block header is exactly 64 bytes at format version 1.0, block start is 64-byte aligned, every field naturally aligned, all fields little-endian. Field offsets fixed: $00 signature (8 bytes), $08/$0A format version major/minor (quarter-words), $0C header length (half-word), $10 total length (half-word), $14 address-map entry size (quarter-word), $16 extension entry size (quarter-word), $18 block address (word), $20 physical memory size (word), $28/$2C address-map offset/count (half-words), $30/$34 extension-list offset/count (half-words), $38/$3A base version major/minor (quarter-words), $3C reserved (half-word).
- Self-describing sizes (header length plus two entry-size fields) as the growth mechanism, with an explicit minor-version-appends-only / major-version-may-break rule and three reader rules that make a 1.0 reader work against every later 1.x block.
- Redundant block-address field at $18 duplicating the pointer CSR, so a snapshot or memory image inspected offline carries its own address and is validatable.
- Address map defined as a complete partition of physical space from address zero upward, ascending, non-overlapping, adjacent, with holes carried as explicit kind-0 entries rather than omitted. 32-byte entries: start (word), length (word), kind (half-word), attributes (half-word), reserved (word).
- Five address-map kind values: #0 unpopulated hole, #1 free populated memory, #2 the boot-information block's own storage, #3 the loaded image (boot ROM or raw program), #4 VM-reserved. All other values reserved, and a reader treats an unrecognized kind as unavailable rather than guessing.
- Two address-map attribute bits: bit 0 in-use at entry to the first instruction, bit 1 reclaimable after software finishes with the contents. All other bits zero.
- Extension-list entry layout, 32 bytes: 16-byte zero-padded ASCII name (no terminator at 16 characters), quarter-word major and minor versions, half-word opcode-page field holding the escape byte zero-extended or $FFFFFFFF when the extension allocates no page, word reserved.
- Extension-list ordering rule: the first entry is always named 'base' with the header's base version and an opcode-page value of $FFFFFFFF; later entries are ordered ascending by escape byte, with page-less extensions last, so two machines are byte-comparable.
- Cross-consistency invariants made normative and testable: the header's physical memory size equals the sum of non-hole region lengths, and the feature bitmap CSR and the extension list agree in both directions.
- Named the discovery register 'the boot-information pointer' by role only, declared it read-only (a write raises the illegal-operand trap), and deferred its CSR number and the reset state to the privileged-architecture chapter.
- Out-of-range and hole accesses are stated to fault and report the offending address WITHOUT inventing a trap-cause name, deferring the cause name to the trap-model chapter. Flagged below as a possible naming coordination point.
- Address arithmetic wraps modulo 2^64 rather than faulting on the arithmetic, with accessibility treated as the separate question.
- Misaligned access that crosses a page boundary is one architectural access that may fault on either page and leaves register and memory unmodified, so software never observes a half-completed misaligned access.
- Natural alignment defined as address a multiple of access width, and single-copy atomicity scoped to aligned accesses of 8 bytes or fewer; block-memory instructions explicitly carry no atomicity guarantee of any kind.
- Instruction-fetch coherence extended explicitly to partial overwrites of an instruction and to a store to the immediately following instruction, with the cost placed on the implementation (decoded-instruction caches and translators must self-invalidate).
- Devices-are-not-in-memory stated with two testable consequences (no device carve-out in the address map, no memory access with a non-memory side effect) and the trade named honestly (ports need their own instructions and a device with a large data window transfers through a port).
- Assembly example uses ';' line comments, matching the documented mazm comment syntax, and deliberately reads the entry size from the header so the loop survives a minor version bump.
- Implementation-notes section adds one item beyond the brief's list: the base's silence about the upper address bits is recorded as deliberate headroom for the capability pointer format.

## extensions, versioning, conformance

- Extension identity contents: I fixed that a ratified extension carries a name, a version, exactly one escape page, one contiguous CSR range (possibly empty), its own spec chapter, and its own conformance-suite section. The record fixes name/version/page/CSR-range only; the chapter and suite-section requirements are mine.
- Extension name form: short lowercase identifier in the mnemonic alphabet. `base` names the frozen base and is explicitly not an extension and takes no escape byte.
- Seven pages is a hard ceiling with no second escape level ever; when the seventh is allocated the architecture is out of opcode space. Nothing in the record or the backbone says what happens at exhaustion.
- Deallocation does not exist: a ratified extension keeps its escape byte, CSR range, and bitmap bit permanently even if superseded, so old binaries keep trapping predictably rather than silently decoding as something new.
- Extension dependencies are permitted, must be declared at ratification, and the depended-on extension must be ratified first. The record is silent on inter-extension dependency.
- May/may-not lists: I enumerated them (the record gives only the principle). Additions beyond the prompt's three prohibitions include forbidding a mode bit that changes base behavior, forbidding making base behavior conditional on extension presence, forbidding reassignment of primary-page reserved bytes, and forbidding cross-range CSR access; on the permissive side, extension trap causes drawn from a cause space the trap-model chapter reserves for extensions (this presumes that chapter reserves one; flagged for the trap-model author).
- Discovery split of labor: the feature bitmap answers presence (hot path, one bit per allocated escape page, read-only, present and all-zero in allocated bits on a base-only machine); the boot-information extension list answers name plus version (read once at startup). I deliberately did not fix the bitmap's CSR number or bit assignments (deferred to the privileged-architecture chapter) nor the boot block's layout (deferred to the memory-model chapter).
- Trap-probing for extension presence is stated as permitted but unnecessary, since the trap is defined.
- Base version string: `2.0`, written `2.0-draft` pre-ratification with the suffix simply dropped at ratification; no `2.1` exists and no mechanism to produce one is defined.
- Errata mechanism: a third version component (`2.0.1`) for text corrections that change no conformance test's expected result; not reported by any CSR, not in the boot block, not named in a conformance claim, and observationally invisible to a guest. Nothing in the record covers errata.
- Extension version format `major.minor`, first ratified version `1.0`, monotonic, numbers never reused.
- Minor-increment semantics: strictly additive superset within the same page and CSR range, with the forward/backward compatibility properties stated as testable.
- Major-increment semantics: an incompatible redesign is a NEW extension with a new name (`vec` -> `vec2`), new escape byte, new CSR range, new bitmap bit, and no implicit inheritance. This preserves the invariant that a byte sequence decodes to one operation or to a trap, never to a second incompatible operation.
- Explicit non-promises of version numbers: nothing about performance, nothing about timing, nothing about which extensions are present.
- Conformance claim written form: `Maize base 2.0, vec 1.2, atomic 1.0`, exhaustive extension set, and the claim is self-checkable from inside the guest by cross-asserting the feature bitmap against the boot-block extension list.
- Suite factoring rules: base section must pass identically with and without extensions present; an extension section may use base instructions plus declared dependencies only; absence tests live in the base section, not in extension sections.
- Test observation channel: architectural state at halt (general registers, readable CSRs, guest memory), deliberately not any device surface, so the same binaries certify machines with no device set defined yet. One behavior per test binary.
- Concrete content of the four test categories, including the decode-length construction (instruction under test placed ahead of a known instruction, observe where control lands), the requirement that a trap check also assert no partial result, the specific boundary-value list, and the unmapped-page-mapped-by-handler construction for restartability.
- Reserved/unimplemented reporting: three enumerated failure outcomes, including an explicit call-out that a read-zero/discard-write unimplemented CSR is a failure and that a v1 port is the likely source of it. Also the positive test that the byte following an unimplemented extension's escape byte is never fetched.
- Result reporting: section-level pass/fail with no partial pass; failing an extension section means the machine conforms to the smaller claim, while failing any base test means no conformance claim is available because the base is not divisible.

## instruction-reference-control

- sys syscall-number delivery: the machine places the syscall number (the 8-bit literal, or the low byte of rs with the upper 56 bits ignored) into the trap frame's aux word for BOTH forms. The immediate form otherwise had nowhere to deliver its number. D13's arguments-in-registers and syscall-number-register ABI is untouched, so a v1-shaped quesOS kernel is unaffected; aux is an additional, uniform carrier. This is the single call most worth an operator look.
- sys is trap-class (captures the address of the FOLLOWING instruction), matching v1 cause 7, so trap_return resumes past the sys rather than re-executing it. The inventory did not state the class.
- Check-order precedence stated: the operand-byte form-field check happens during decode (encoding chapter step 4) and therefore precedes the privilege check at execution, so an illegal operand form on a privileged instruction yields the illegal-operand trap, not the privileged-operation trap.
- CSR access-rule ordering fixed as: reserved privilege encoding (%10/%11) first, then privilege level, then read-only bit, then unimplemented number. The inventory listed the four rules without an order.
- A CSR number whose privilege field is %10 or %11 raises the ILLEGAL-OPERAND trap, at every privilege level including supervisor. The inventory said only 'reserved and always trap' without naming the trap.
- csr_read of a narrower register returns defined bits in place with undefined bits zero; csr_write ignores bits the target does not define; per-field reserved-encoding treatment is delegated to the privileged-architecture chapter rather than left open. The base defines no write-only CSR.
- Transfer-target faults: an unmapped or non-executable branch/jump/call target raises a page fault on the FETCH of the target, reporting the target as the faulting address, after the transfer has completed. For call this means r31 already holds the link when the handler runs. The transfer instructions raise no fault of their own.
- Branch/jump/call target arithmetic wraps modulo 2^64; the register forms of jump and call take the whole 64-bit register as an absolute address.
- Assembler target syntax: a branch/jump/call target position accepts either a label (resolved by the assembler to the displacement) or a numeric literal with its mandatory base marker, which IS the displacement. Branch targets carry no @ sigil, since they are not memory operands.
- halt is terminal: no further fetch, no interrupt resumes it, and the register/memory state at the halt is the final observable state (which is what lets a conformance binary end with halt).
- wait_for_interrupt is defined on PENDING, not on enablement: an already-pending interrupt satisfies it immediately; if the pending interrupt is enabled it is taken before the following instruction, and if not, execution simply continues with it still pending. A machine that returns immediately and repeatedly is conforming (the instruction is an idleness hint only).
- trap_return specifics: the pc and status words are restored and the cause and aux words are discarded; a page fault on a frame word abandons the pop with the kernel-stack pointer unchanged so the instruction re-executes cleanly.
- tlb_invalidate_address ignores the in-page offset bits, never consults a page table, and never faults for an unmapped/untranslated/out-of-range address; a machine may invalidate more than named, up to everything.
- Port operands carry NO @ sigil (matching the inventory's bare `port_out rs rp`), and the chapter states why: the sigil marks memory traffic and a port operand touches no memory. The upper 48 bits of the port register are ignored rather than checked, so no port register value is invalid.
- Port ordering: port accesses execute in program order with respect to each other and to memory accesses, and are never merged, split, elided, or speculated. A port read may have device side effects, so it is not safe to repeat; port_in with rd=r0 still performs the access and discards the value.
- Added a chapter-level conformance-notes section (the inventory has none for these families), listing directly testable properties including the -7 self-branch, call r31, and the user-level privileged-operation set.

## instruction-reference-memory

- Entry granularity: one entry per mnemonic, with the bare and displaced addressing forms as two Syntax lines inside it, rather than 22 separate load/store entries. Keeps the reference aligned with the inventory's mnemonic list and avoids duplicating identical Operation prose.
- Traps-label scope: the chapter states once that the Traps label names execution-stage traps only, and that the encoding-layer illegal-operand trap (nonzero form field in a plain slot, out-of-range slice index in a sliced slot) applies to every entry without being repeated. Without this, every entry in the chapter would have to carry it.
- Bare addressing is defined as the displaced form with a displacement of zero, so a single effective-address rule covers both forms.
- Address wrap is defined explicitly as a defined outcome rather than a fault: the sum of base and sign-extended displacement is taken modulo 2^64, an access may straddle the top of the address space and continue at address zero, and each byte of such an access is translated on its own.
- Page-fault cleanness extended to the multi-page case: an access crossing a page boundary is ONE access for fault purposes, no byte of a faulting store is written even when the first page is present and writable, and when several bytes are inaccessible the fault reports the LOWEST inaccessible address in the access (so the reported address is a function of the access, not of an implementation's byte order). The inventory says only that memory is unmodified.
- Stores are stated to modify no register and to read nothing back; loads whose destination register is also the base register are stated to overwrite the address after a successful access.
- Slice numbering fixed explicitly from the least significant end (rN.b0 = bits 7..0, rN.q0 = bits 15..0, rN.h1 = bits 63..32). The inventory names the forms but never pins the numbering direction.
- Extract/insert with the same register in both operand slots is defined as read-source-then-write-destination.
- Bitfield immediates are unsigned 8-bit; the inventory's trap rule (width zero, or position plus width above 64) is stated to subsume a position of 64 or more, and bitfield_insert is stated to ignore bits of the source at or above the width so a caller need not mask.
- Block memory: the three register operands must name three DISTINCT registers, and an encoding naming a register twice raises the illegal-operand trap (r0 included). Neither the record nor the backbone addressed operand aliasing, and a shared register cannot carry the restart state. This is the poka-yoke stance applied to operand encoding.
- Block memory restart contract given its exact invariant: at every interruptible point the count register holds the bytes not yet transferred and each pointer register holds the LOWEST address in its region not yet transferred. That phrasing covers both a low-to-high implementation (pointers advance, count decrements) and a high-to-low one (count alone decrements), which is what the inventory's implementation-chosen-order sentence requires, while still being a single testable predicate.
- Block memory completion state fixed uniformly for every implementation and both directions of travel: count zero and each pointer at its original value plus the original count. This makes re-execution of a completed block instruction a harmless no-operation and removes the direction-dependent ambiguity the remaining-work invariant would otherwise leave at count zero.
- Interrupt granularity declared implementation-chosen (an implementation that transfers eight bytes at a time is conforming), with the register invariant required to hold at whatever points interruption is possible.
- block_copy_forward and block_set are declared architecturally ascending, so their mid-operation register state is a unique value rather than a constraint; only block_copy leaves the direction open.
- Block memory pointer arithmetic wraps modulo 2^64, matching the displaced-load rule.
- Single-copy atomicity (from the memory-architecture decision) is stated not to extend across a block-memory operation: the aligned access inside it is atomic on its own, and no two bytes of the transfer are guaranteed to be observed together.
- block_set reads only bits 7..0 of the value register and leaves that register unmodified across an interruption as well as after completion.
- Select: a false condition writes no register at all (not a write of the old value), and both source and condition are read before the destination is written, so any operand aliasing is defined.
- Nine conformance-note bullets added at the chapter end, each phrased as a directly executable check, matching the style of the encoding chapter's conformance section.

## floating-point

- FCSR bit layout: bits 7..5 are the rounding-mode field frm, bits 4..0 are the sticky flags fflags, bits 63..8 are reserved. Neither the decision record nor the backbone fixed a layout; I kept v1's (RISC-V-shaped) low byte so the port is mechanical.
- Reserved FCSR bits reject rather than ignore: a csr_write of a nonzero value into bits 63..8 raises the illegal-operand trap, and a read returns zero there. Chosen for the poka-yoke stance rather than v1's ignore-the-upper-bits behavior, so software built against a future trap-enable extension cannot silently no-op on a base machine.
- Rounding-mode encodings: %000 rne, %001 rtz, %010 rdn, %011 rup, %100 rmm, with %101, %110, and %111 all reserved and trapping on a rounding operation. v1's DYN slot at %111 is simply reserved in v2, since there is no per-instruction rounding field to be dynamic about.
- The reserved-mode trap is scoped to rounding operations only. I enumerated the nineteen rounding operations explicitly and stated that negate, absolute, min, max, all twelve compares, float_widen, and all four float-to-integer conversions execute normally under a reserved mode.
- FLOAT-TO-INTEGER ROUNDING DIRECTION (the largest call): float_to_signed, float_to_unsigned, and their .h forms round toward zero ALWAYS, as a property of the opcode, ignoring frm. v1 rounded these per FRM. I changed it because a C cast would otherwise cost two csr accesses around every conversion, and because round-toward-zero convert is the single native host instruction on both JIT targets (cvttsd2si, fcvtzs). I documented the compensating idiom (add and subtract 2^52 of the value's sign to round to integral under the current mode, then truncate). Integer-to-float still rounds per frm.
- Tininess for the underflow flag is detected AFTER rounding, and uf is raised only when the tiny result is also inexact. Pinned because IEEE leaves the choice to the implementation and a conformance test needs one answer; after-rounding matches both x86 and AArch64.
- Overflow always raises nx alongside of, in every rounding direction.
- float_to_unsigned of a negative value strictly between -1 and 0 truncates to zero and raises nx rather than nv, since zero is its exact truncation; only a value at or below -1 saturates with nv.
- Minimum and maximum follow IEEE 754-2008 minNum/maxNum (a lone quiet NaN yields the numeric operand; two NaNs yield the canonical quiet NaN; a signaling NaN raises nv and is then treated as its quiet counterpart for selection), not the 754-2019 minimum/maximum operations that propagate NaNs. Named explicitly in the text as a divergence from the standard this chapter otherwise claims, since the chapter's conformance scope says 754-2019.
- float_maximum resolves the zero pair to positive zero, the mirror of the inventory's stated -0 < +0 rule for float_minimum.
- float_widen is exact and raises no nx or uf (every binary32 subnormal becomes a binary64 normal), does not consult frm, and therefore has no rounding-mode trap. float_narrow rounds and does.
- Both format conversions canonicalize NaNs: a NaN through float_narrow or float_widen comes out as the destination format's canonical quiet NaN, with nv raised only for a signaling NaN. The chapter's payload-preserving exception is confined to float_negate and float_absolute.
- Fused operands are all read before the destination is written, so naming a source register as the destination is well defined.
- Canonical quiet NaN patterns pinned as $7FF8000000000000 (binary64) and $000000007FC00000 written into the register (binary32), carried over from v1.
- Assembly spelling of the FCSR in examples: `csr_read fcsr r5` and `csr_write r5 fcsr`, using a symbolic register name with no numeric base marker (the mandatory-base rule governs numeric literals, and this is a symbol). The privileged-architecture chapter owns the actual number and the symbol table; if that chapter spells it differently, my three-line example needs a one-word edit.
- Per-entry Traps lines omit the encoding-layer illegal-operand trap for a nonzero form field. I stated once in the family preamble that every operand slot in the chapter is a plain slot, so the trap applies uniformly and the entries name only format-specific traps.
- Predicate completeness is argued as fourteen C-reachable relational predicates, with the negations spelled out (`xor rd #1 rd` after float_compare_lt for 'not less than', and float_compare_ne combined with float_compare_ordered by `and` for ordered-and-unequal).
- Conformance-scope section names four parts of 754 that are outside the base: alternate exception handling, formats other than binary32 and binary64, per-instruction rounding, and roundToIntegral / remainder / scaleB / logB / transcendentals. The last is a genuine gap the base has no instruction for, and I stated the software idiom rather than proposing an opcode.

## abi

- Callee-saved partition: r20 through r29, exactly ten registers, chosen as a decade-aligned block so the whole map reads r2-r9 arguments, r10-r19 temporaries, r20-r29 saved, r30/r31 pointers.
- ABI names: zero (r0), tp (r1), a0-a7 (r2-r9), t0-t9 (r10-r19), s0-s8 (r20-r28), fp (r29), sp (r30), ra (r31). None of these were fixed by the record or the backbone.
- r1 role: thread pointer, invariant across calls, never an argument, never compiler-allocated. The record left r1 unassigned; the alternative (a link-editor scratch register) was rejected because nothing in the encoding needs a relaxation scratch.
- r29 is the frame pointer and doubles as a ninth saved register when no frame pointer is used, putting fp/sp/ra in one adjacent cluster at the top of the file. Frame pointer use is optional.
- fp holds the frame address, defined as the value sp had at function entry, rather than pointing at the saved-fp slot. Saved ra at @fp-$8 and saved caller fp at @fp-$10, giving a metadata-free backtrace chain.
- Frame region order fixed as incoming stack arguments, saved ra then saved fp, other saved registers, locals and spills, outgoing argument area at sp. The outgoing area is allocated once in the prologue so sp is never adjusted around an individual call.
- Narrow scalars are held ZERO-extended in registers and in stack slots, for signed and unsigned types alike, because every .h instruction already zero-extends. Documented cost: a signed half-word comparison must widen each operand with extract.sh, since the compare and branch families have no .h forms.
- Aggregate classification thresholds: 8 bytes or fewer in one register, 9 to 16 bytes in two consecutive registers, larger by reference to a caller-allocated copy. Contents follow the little-endian memory image; unused high bytes of the last register are defined zero rather than unspecified.
- Register-exhaustion rules: an argument that does not fit in the remaining registers goes entirely to the stack (never split), and once any argument goes to the stack every later argument does too (no back-fill). This makes classification a pure function of the argument list.
- Empty aggregates consume no register and no stack slot.
- Stack argument slots are 8 bytes (16 for 16-byte-aligned types), the caller writes the WHOLE slot with the value extended into it, and trailing pad bytes of an odd-sized argument are zero.
- Large-result convention: hidden pointer as a real first argument in a0 shifting all declared arguments right, and the callee returns the same pointer in a0. A void function leaves a0/a1 undefined by the ABI.
- Varargs: the ENTIRE variadic tail goes on the stack regardless of how many argument registers remain (reading the record's 'varargs entirely on the stack' literally), so there is no register save area and va_list is a single word pointer. Consequence noted: a variadic call needs the prototype in scope, which C already requires.
- va_start = frame address plus the byte count of fixed arguments that landed on the stack (usually zero, so it is a capture of the incoming sp); va_arg loads and advances by whole 8-byte slots; va_copy copies the word; va_end emits nothing. Default promotions: sub-int integers to int zero-extended into the slot, float to double, aggregates over 16 bytes by reference in one slot.
- Syscall register contract: every general register except a0 is preserved across sys, which is strictly stronger than the function-call contract. Derived from the trap model's 'the kernel saves only what it uses' plus 'arguments stay live across the boundary'; rationale and the translator benefit are stated in the chapter.
- Dynamic syscall numbers travel in t0 by convention; the number itself rides the sys instruction operand rather than an argument register, leaving all eight argument registers free.
- Syscall boundary details: the user sp is untouched, the kernel imposes no minimum free space below sp, the kernel does not read the user stack as part of the convention, and a syscall returning nothing still writes a defined value into a0.
- No red zone stated explicitly with its consequence (trap, signal, and debugger writes below sp), and sp is required aligned and valid at ALL instruction boundaries rather than only at call sites, because a trap can inspect the stack at any point.
- LP64 type mapping stated in this chapter because classification depends on sizes: plain char is signed, long double is binary64 (the machine has no wider format), _Bool is one byte holding 0 or 1.
- Floating-point arguments and results carry no separate class: binary64 as the full-word bit pattern, binary32 in the low half-word with the upper half zero (no NaN-boxing, matching the machine's zero-extension rule).
- Conformance framed honestly: the machine enforces none of the ABI, so the section lists two-sided checkable properties, and notes the syscall preservation rule is the only one a machine (its kernel) can fail on its own.
- The 16-byte alignment is explained as headroom for a future vector extension so no flag day is needed, per the decision's parenthetical.

## device-surface, boot, appendix-c-syscall-surface

- Port-space map redesigned as regular 16-port blocks: block 0 ($0000-$000F) is a machine block (port $0000 identification word with magic $4D32 in the low quarter-word plus base spec version, port $0001 class-presence bitmap), and each device class owns the block at class code times sixteen. v1's ad-hoc pinout ($00 console, $10 keyboard, $20 block, $40 timer, $50 framebuffer) is not carried forward.
- Port ranges partitioned: $0000-$007F standard classes, $0080-$7FFF reserved for future specification-assigned classes, $8000-$FFFF available for implementation-specific devices with no conformance coverage. v1 only said 'reserved low block plus a convention'.
- Common class skeleton invented so probing, status, and interrupt control are uniform: offset 0 identification (class code plus class contract version), offset 1 status on read and acknowledge on write, offset 2 interrupt control, offsets 3-15 class-specific. Status bits 0 primary condition, 1 busy, 2 invalid-request are common; 5 overrun and 6 transfer-error are used consistently where classes need them.
- Presence and mandatory set changed from v1: only console and timer are required, every other class is optional, and an absent class leaves its whole block unpopulated (read zero, discard write) so presence is detectable with one port_in. v1 mandated five devices including framebuffer and block storage, which is unworkable for a headless machine.
- Port transfer width: v2 port_in/port_out move a whole word, a narrower device register reads zero-extended and ignores high bits on write. v1 carried the operand's sub-register width, which has no v2 equivalent.
- Interrupt lines: each class owns exactly one line, line index equals class code, and the mapping from line index to cause number plus all masking and acknowledgement mechanics are delegated to the trap-model chapter. v1's fixed IRQ vector numbers (32-36) are not carried forward, since v2's cause numbering is that chapter's to fix.
- Bulk-transfer rule tightened into four explicit conditions plus a defined non-transferring failure (invalid-request status bit, no partial transfer, no trap), and registered buffers are stated to be physical addresses with the device holding no translation state.
- Keyboard generalized off v1's PC Set-1 (XT) scancodes, which are app-shaped (DOOM-era) machinery. A key event is now a quarter-word USB HID keyboard/keypad usage-page code plus a press/release bit in bit 16, with usage id zero as the not-an-event value, plus an overrun bit for a bounded event queue.
- Framebuffer generalized off v1's 8-slot registration table and $FFFFFFFF console sentinel. The surface count is reported by a read-only port and is at least one, offset 10 selects the scanned-out surface, a claim the machine cannot honor sets invalid-request per claim, and the console-sentinel and switch-back-history behavior is dropped as host presentation policy.
- Block storage given a concrete port-level contract v1 left reserved: block size and capacity read-only ports, block number, buffer base, length in blocks, and a command port (1 read, 2 write), with asynchronous completion via busy plus transfer-complete and a transfer-error bit.
- Network and entropy classes defined from scratch (v1 pinned neither). Network is whole-frame transmit/receive through registered guest-memory buffers with a maximum-frame-length port and an overrun bit, and says nothing above the link layer. Entropy is a single data port with data-available status, with source quality declared in the conformance claim rather than mandated.
- Timer given nanosecond period and a monotonic nanosecond counter port, with delivered resolution left as an implementation property and period-write-takes-effect-at-next-expiry stated. v1's period was in instruction ticks, which is implementation-shaped.
- Reset address fixed architecturally at physical $0000000000001000, identical for every artifact form, with a structured artifact placing a jump there rather than the machine reading an entry point. v1 used $0 for flat images and the recorded entry for .mzx.
- Initial register state: all thirty-two general registers zero, including r30 and r31, so the machine chooses no stack for the guest (v1 pointed SP at a process-start block). The boot-information block is reachable only through its CSR, not duplicated in a register.
- Initial CSR state fixed for the registers that determine whether the machine runs: paging root zero and translation disabled, trap-vector zero, kernel-stack zero, interrupts disabled, round-to-nearest-ties-to-even with clear sticky flags, syscall-provider bit clear, feature bitmap read-only, boot-information-block address populated. Per-register reset values are delegated to the privileged-architecture chapter.
- Paging off at reset is stated as translation not being performed at all, rather than an identity mapping, so no page table is consulted and no page fault is possible before the guest turns translation on.
- The machine zeroes every byte of usable physical memory the artifact did not fill, making early guest BSS clearing unnecessary and preventing cross-run leakage. This touches the memory-model chapter's territory and may want reconciliation with it.
- Post-reset the machine holds no resident firmware: no callable service, no reserved address, no callback. Stated as an exhaustive boundary alongside an exhaustive list of what the loaded software owns.
- User level is reachable only via trap_return with a hand-built frame, stated here as the single privilege-descent mechanism, with frame layout delegated to the trap-model chapter.
- Syscall appendix reframed as normative-boundary prose with no number table at all, and says plainly that v1's table was reference-implementation policy that readers mistook for a machine contract.

## assembler

- Label definitions stand alone on their own line and never prefix an instruction. Rationale given in the text: one grammar production per line, and the instruction column does not shift when a label is added or removed.
- Comments are semicolon-to-end-of-line only (carried from v1's mazm tokenizer). No block comments, no nested comments, no line-continuation character.
- The dot is removed from the identifier alphabet (v1's mazm accepted '.' inside identifiers). Identifiers are letter-or-underscore followed by letters, digits, underscores. This is what makes rN.bK structurally distinct from a label with no reserved-name list.
- Digit separators: the underscore is the ONLY separator, legal between two digits. v1's back-tick and comma separators are retired. The comma is retired deliberately so that a comma-separated operand list from another assembler is a hard error rather than silently ignored whitespace.
- Sign placement inside a literal follows the marker ($-1, #-24), matching the inventory chapter's own example 'compare_lt_unsigned r4 $-1 r5'. A leading '+' is accepted.
- Character literals ('A', with escapes) are admitted and argued not to violate the mandatory-base rule, because they name a value by identity rather than by digits in an unstated base. A code point above 255 is a diagnostic.
- Escape set for string and character literals is fixed at \\, \", \', \n, \r, \t, \0, and \xHH; an unrecognized escape is a diagnostic.
- Expressions contain NO whitespace. Whitespace terminates an operand, so `$10+#2` is one operand and `$10 + #2` is a diagnostic. This is what lets operands stay comma-free with no ambiguity about operand boundaries.
- Expression operator set: | ^ & << >> + - * / with unary - and ~ and parentheses; 64-bit two's complement wrapping; >> is arithmetic. There is deliberately NO remainder operator, because '%' is the binary base marker and a second meaning would reintroduce exactly the ambiguity the markers exist to prevent.
- Relocatable expressions are limited to four forms: a symbol; symbol plus constant; symbol minus constant; symbol minus symbol in the same section of the same module (folds to a constant). Everything else naming an unresolved symbol is a diagnostic.
- Range checking is exact: a value outside the field's range is a diagnostic and the assembler never truncates.
- Labels may be forward-referenced; constants defined by `constant` must be defined before use. Justified by the no-relaxation property (instruction length depends only on mnemonic plus operand syntax, so all addresses are fixed on the first pass).
- Reserved symbol `here` names the address of the first byte of the current statement; a program may not define it.
- Directives are bare lowercase reserved words with no sigil and never begin with a dot (the dot stays reserved for length/format specifiers and slices).
- Placement model: both a section model (`section code|rodata|data|bss`) for relocatable objects and an `origin` directive for flat images, with mixing the two in one module a diagnostic. Adapted from v1's SECTION plus ADDRESS/CODE.
- `align` takes a power-of-two constant; padding is `nop` bytes in the code section and zero bytes elsewhere; a non-power-of-two or zero alignment is a diagnostic.
- Data-emission directive names use full literal-word widths: data_byte, data_quarter_word, data_half_word, data_word, plus data_string, data_string_zero, data_fill, and reserve. Each integer directive takes a whitespace-separated list.
- v1's separate reference-emitting directive (DREF) is folded away: data_half_word and data_word accept a relocatable expression and emit the corresponding absolute relocation. The two narrower directives take constant expressions only.
- Symbol definition directive is spelled `constant name expr` (v1's LABEL); redefinition is a diagnostic.
- Linkage keeps v1's `global` and `extern` (v1's PUBLIC synonym is dropped, one canonical name); an extern of a locally defined name is harmless and the local definition wins.
- INCLUDE IS KEPT, as `include "path"`, resolved relative to the including file, with cycles a diagnostic. Justification recorded in the text: a constant is not a linkable symbol, so extern cannot carry syscall numbers, port numbers, or CSR numbers between modules.
- Branch/jump/call/pc_add target slots read a symbol as an address (assembler computes the displacement) and a numeric literal as the raw displacement, emitted unconverted. The distinction is purely lexical and is safe only because base markers are mandatory.
- An out-of-range displacement is a diagnostic; the assembler performs no branch relaxation and no long-branch expansion.
- Cross-section and extern targets emit a placeholder plus a 32-bit program-counter-relative relocation; the relocation's numbering and name are deferred to the object-format chapter rather than invented here.
- Mnemonic selection is enumerated as the single sanctioned convenience (move register vs 64-bit immediate vs the six narrow dotted forms, load/store bare vs displaced, ALU/compare register vs immediate, jump/call/sys register vs immediate), with `@rb+#0` selecting the displaced form deliberately.
- Diagnostics model: no warning class at all; every condition is accepted or rejected, one diagnostic suppresses all output, and the assembler continues in order to report further diagnostics in the same run.
- Round-trip property stated as a testable contract: the disassembler emits this language and its output reassembles byte-for-byte, with canonical spellings (lowercase mnemonics, uppercase hex digits, ABI aliases).
- Sliced operands are required, not optional, in sliced slots: `extract.zb r3 r7` is a diagnostic rather than a shorthand for byte 0.
- Register spelling admits no leading zero (`r05` is a diagnostic).
- Worked example (word_to_hex plus byte_to_hex) keeps all loop state in the stack frame and reloads after the call, so it depends on no unratified callee-saved register split; it uses only the ratified 16-byte alignment, no-red-zone, r2-r9 argument, ra, and sp facts.

## trap-model, privileged-architecture

- Cause numbering: 0 illegal instruction, 1 illegal operand, 2 divide error, 3 breakpoint, 4 privileged operation, 5 and 6 held reserved and never delivered (v1's segment-bounds and stack causes, dark so a v1 handler table cannot silently alias), 7 syscall (v1 number kept per the syscall-continuity decision), 8 page fault on fetch, 9 page fault on load, 10 page fault on store, 11..31 reserved for future synchronous causes, 32..255 external interrupts.
- Page faults split into three causes by access kind (fetch/load/store) instead of v1's single cause 8, with subcode 0 = no valid mapping and 1 = permission violation. This lets a kernel vector directly on access kind.
- Subcodes assigned: illegal operand 0..4 (undefined form field, invalid immediate, reserved FP rounding mode, unimplemented CSR number, write to read-only CSR); divide error 0/1 (divide-by-zero, quotient overflow); page faults 0/1 (no mapping, permission).
- Frame layout at ascending offsets from the (post-decrement) trap-stack register: +0 pc, +8 status, +16 cause, +24 aux. Frame is 32 bytes; the trap-stack register is full-descending, hardware-decremented on entry and incremented by trap_return, and requires 16-byte alignment (misaligned csr_write raises illegal operand). No general register, including r30, is touched by hardware; nested frames stack beneath, and a handler that uses the trap stack as its working stack moves the CSR down itself.
- Status word layout: bits 1:0 privilege (%00 user, %01 supervisor, %10/%11 reserved), bit 2 external-interrupt enable, bits 63:3 reserved written zero. The frame's status word is a snapshot of the live status CSR, and it is the only 'previous privilege' mechanism; trap_return restores it after total validation (reserved privilege encoding or any reserved bit set raises illegal operand and changes nothing, including the trap-stack register).
- Cause word packing carried forward from v1: cause in bits 7:0, subcode in bits 15:8, bits 63:16 reserved written zero.
- Aux per cause: offending byte/value for the decode-class faults and privileged-operation, faulting virtual address (exact, not page base) for the three page faults, the syscall number zero-extended for cause 7, zero for breakpoint, divide error, and interrupts.
- Syscall aux carries the syscall number, which v1 could not do because the number was register-borne only. Arguments stay in r2..r9 and the result in r2/r3 per the calling convention; this is additive to v1's shape, not a replacement.
- Vector table: 256 entries of 8 bytes holding full 64-bit virtual handler addresses, relocatable via a trap_vector_base CSR (v1's fixed $1000 base is dropped), 2 KiB-aligned with a misaligned write raising illegal operand. Vector fetch happens BEFORE the frame push so an uninstalled handler halts with state untouched.
- Delivery sequence fixed and observable: determine cause, fetch vector, halt if zero, push frame, set supervisor and clear interrupt-enable, jump. Paging root unchanged across entry, so the handler runs in the interrupted address space.
- No-handler halt and double fault are surfaced through a new read-only halt_cause CSR (bits 7:0 cause, 15:8 subcode, 17:16 kind where 0 = halt instruction, 1 = no handler, 2 = double fault). Double fault is defined narrowly as a page fault raised by the vector read or by any of the four frame stores.
- Interrupt state model: four 64-bit read-only pending CSRs and four read-write enable CSRs covering causes 0..255, with bits 0..31 of enable0 required to be zero (write of a 1 raises illegal operand) because synchronous causes are unmaskable. Global gate is status.ie. Lowest-numbered deliverable cause wins (deterministic and testable), pending bit cleared by the machine before the frame is built.
- wait_for_interrupt completes on pending-and-enabled regardless of status.ie, so a masked idle loop that polls the pending registers is a defined arrangement.
- Interrupts are taken between instructions and additionally at the block-memory instructions' defined mid-operation boundaries, where the captured pc is the block instruction's own address.
- CSR numbering (16-bit layout from the inventory): $0000 fcsr (user RW); $2000 feature_bitmap (user RO); $4000 status, $4001 trap_stack, $4002 trap_vector_base, $4003 paging_root, $4004..$4007 interrupt_enable0..3, $4008 syscall_provider (supervisor RW); $6000..$6003 interrupt_pending0..3, $6004 halt_cause, $6005 boot_info (supervisor RO). Index space $0000..$0FFF is the base's, $1000..$1FFF is allocated to extensions in blocks of $100.
- No faulting-address or fault-error CSR exists (v1's FAULT_VA/FAULT_ERR are dropped), because the frame carries address, access kind, presence-vs-permission, and interrupted privilege. Stated explicitly, with the nested-fault-destroys-outer-description hazard as the reason.
- No cycle or retired-instruction counter (metering extension) and no hart id (single-hart base), stated as deliberate absences.
- Reset values for the registers this chapter owns: all writable base CSRs zero except status = $1 (supervisor, interrupts disabled); feature_bitmap and boot_info populated before the first instruction; halt_cause and the pending registers zero. Deferred to the execution-model chapter for the full reset contract.
- paging_root layout: bits 3:0 mode (0 bare, 1 Sv48, 2..15 reserved), bits 11:4 reserved, bits 63:12 root physical address. A reserved mode or a nonzero reserved bit raises illegal operand on write, where v1 silently forced the bits to zero and treated an unknown mode as bare. Poka-yoke change, flagged in prose as a deliberate deviation from v1's forcing behavior.
- PTE format restated from the v1 reference walk (the v1 memory-model chapter does not contain it; the fault conditions in the v1 trap-model chapter and src/cpu.cpp sv48_walk are the sources): bit 0 V, 1 R, 2 W, 3 X, 4 U, 5 G, 6 A, 7 D, bits 11:8 software-available, bits 63:12 physical address bits. Hardware reads V/R/W/X/U and the address only, never writes an entry, and never inspects G/A/D.
- Translation-cache staleness is defined as bounded nondeterminism rather than undefined behavior: without an invalidation the machine may use any translation the tables held since the last invalidation for that address, and the broken party is the program. Cached translations carry their leaf permission bits and are re-checked per access, so a privilege change needs no flush.
- The seven privileged instructions are enumerated as a closed set: trap_return, halt, wait_for_interrupt, tlb_invalidate_all, tlb_invalidate_address, port_in, port_out. sys and breakpoint are explicitly not privileged.
